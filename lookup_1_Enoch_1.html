Non-Canonical: Ethiopian Orthodox · 1 Enoch
1 Enoch 2

Biblical Text

v. The Book of Enoch (R.H. Charles 1917) (RHCEnoch)
1 The words of the blessing of Enoch, wherewith he blessed the elect ⌈⌈and⌉⌉ righteous, who will be living in the day of tribulation, when all the wicked ⌈⌈and godless⌉⌉ are to be removed.
2 And he took up his parable and said—Enoch a righteous man, whose eyes were opened by God, saw the vision of the Holy One in the heavens, ⌈which⌉ the angels showed me, and from them I heard everything, and from them I understood as I saw, but not for this generation, but for a remote one which is for to come.
3 Concerning the elect I said, and took up ⌈my⌉ parable concerning them: The Holy Great One will come forth from His dwelling,
4 And the eternal God will tread upon the earth, (even) on Mount Sinai, [And appear from His camp] And appear in the strength of His might from the heaven ⌈of heavens⌉.
5 And all shall be smitten with fear, And the Watchers shall quake, And great fear and trembling shall seize them unto the ends of the earth.
6 And the high mountains shall be shaken, And the high hills shall be made low, And shall melt like wax before the flame.
7 And the earth shall be ⌈wholly⌉ rent in sunder, And all that is upon the earth shall perish, And there shall be a judgement upon all (men).
8 But with the righteous He will make peace. And will protect the elect, And mercy shall be upon them. And they shall all belong to God, And they shall be prospered, And they shall ⌈all⌉ be blessed. ⌈And He will help them all⌉, And light shall appear unto them, ⌈And He will make peace with them⌉.
9 And behold! He cometh with ten thousands of ⌈His⌉ holy ones To execute judgement upon all, And to destroy ⌈all⌉ the ungodly: And to convict all flesh Of all the works ⌈of their ungodliness⌉ which they have ungodly committed, ⌈And of all the hard things which⌉ ungodly sinners ⌈have spoken⌉ against Him.

Study Questions

Challenge assumptions, probe reasoning, expose tensions

Challenge Assumptions
  1. What assumptions do we make about divine revelation when we accept or reject Enoch's claim in verse 2 that angels showed him visions from God?
  2. How might our modern discomfort with the stark binary between 'elect' and 'wicked' throughout this passage reflect contemporary assumptions about human nature and divine justice?
  3. What presuppositions about the relationship between physical and spiritual reality underlie our interpretation of the cosmic upheaval described in verses 5-7?
Probe Reasoning
  1. If God truly desires to 'make peace' with the righteous as verse 8 claims, what logical relationship exists with verse 7's declaration that 'all that is upon the earth shall perish'?
  2. What reasoning supports Enoch's authority to speak blessing over future generations (v1) based on a vision intended for 'a remote generation' (v2)?
  3. How do we logically reconcile verse 8's emphasis on God's mercy and protection with verse 9's focus on destruction and judgment - are these contradictory or complementary?
Expose Tensions
  1. What tension exists between the universal scope of destruction in verse 7 ('all that is upon the earth shall perish') and the preservation of the elect described in verse 8?
  2. How do we resolve the apparent contradiction between verse 5's claim that 'all shall be smitten with fear' and verse 8's promise of peace for the righteous?
  3. What theological tensions arise from verse 9's fourfold repetition of 'ungodly' - does this suggest degrees of wickedness or emphasize total moral corruption?

Observe → Interpret → Apply

Observe
  1. What specific titles does Enoch use for God in verses 3-4, and what might each reveal about God's character?
  2. How many times does the word 'all' appear in verses 5-9, and what does this repetition emphasize?
  3. What is the sequence of events Enoch describes from God's coming forth (v3) to the final judgment (v9)?
  4. What different sources of Enoch's knowledge does verse 2 mention, and how does he validate his authority?
Interpret
  1. Why does Enoch specify that his blessing is for those 'living in the day of tribulation' rather than all righteous people throughout history?
  2. What is the significance of the Watchers quaking in verse 5, and who are these beings in the broader context of 1 Enoch?
  3. How does the imagery of wax melting before flame (v6) function as a metaphor for divine judgment?
  4. What does verse 8's promise that the righteous 'shall all belong to God' suggest about their current spiritual status?
Apply
  1. How should modern believers prepare for living faithfully during times when 'great fear and trembling' seize the earth (v5)?
  2. What practical steps can Christians take to ensure they are among those with whom God 'will make peace' (v8)?
  3. How does the certainty of God's judgment on 'ungodly sinners' (v9) motivate evangelism and personal holiness today?
  4. In what ways can believers experience God's protective mercy (v8) while still living in a world destined for judgment?

Six cognitive levels from recall to creation

Remember
  1. According to verse 2, what three sources provided Enoch with his visionary knowledge?
  2. What specific location does verse 4 mention as where 'the eternal God will tread upon the earth'?
  3. List the different names or titles used for God throughout verses 3-9.
Understand
  1. Explain the progression from Enoch's blessing (v1) to his parable (v2-3) to his description of divine judgment (v4-9).
  2. What is the relationship between the cosmic upheaval described in verses 5-7 and the human judgment described in verse 9?
  3. How do verses 8 and 9 present two contrasting destinies for different groups of people?
Apply
  1. How would you use Enoch's distinction between 'elect and righteous' versus 'wicked and godless' to counsel someone questioning their spiritual status?
  2. What guidance would you draw from verse 8's promises to help believers facing persecution or hardship?
  3. How might verse 2's emphasis on revelation 'for a remote generation' inform how we approach prophetic literature today?
Analyze
  1. Compare the literary structure of verses 1-2 (introduction) with verses 3-9 (vision content) - how does Enoch establish authority before delivering his message?
  2. Examine the cause-and-effect relationships in verses 4-7: what triggers the cosmic fear and geological upheaval?
  3. What elements in this passage suggest it belongs to the apocalyptic genre rather than wisdom or historical literature?
Evaluate
  1. Assess the theological implications of verse 8's promise that the elect 'shall all belong to God' - does this suggest universal salvation for the righteous or predestination?
  2. Critique Enoch's claim in verse 2 to have received direct angelic revelation - what criteria should modern readers use to evaluate such assertions?
  3. Judge whether the stark dualism between righteous and wicked throughout this passage adequately reflects the complexity of human moral experience.
Create
  1. Write a modern paraphrase of verses 8-9 that captures both the comfort for believers and warning for unbelievers while addressing contemporary concerns about divine justice.
  2. Design a comparative chart showing how Enoch's theophany in verses 4-7 relates to similar divine appearances in canonical texts like Exodus 19, Isaiah 64, or Habakkuk 3.
  3. Compose a series of reflection questions that help small group members wrestle with living as 'elect and righteous' in a morally ambiguous world.

Academic approach: genre, history, language, structure

Genre & Structure
  1. How does the opening formula 'The words of the blessing of Enoch' in verse 1 function within the apocalyptic genre to establish textual authority?
  2. What structural purpose does verse 2 serve as a transition from the blessing introduction to the visionary content in verses 3-9?
  3. How do the literary devices of parallelism and repetition in verses 8-9 create emphasis and contrast between the fate of righteous and ungodly?
Historical Context
  1. What does verse 4's reference to Mount Sinai suggest about the author's understanding of divine revelation and covenant history?
  2. How might the 'Watchers' mentioned in verse 5 reflect Second Temple period angelology and concerns about cosmic rebellion?
  3. What historical circumstances during the Hellenistic or Maccabean periods might have prompted the sharp distinction between 'elect' and 'wicked' throughout this passage?
  4. How does verse 2's claim that this vision is for 'a remote generation' reflect typical apocalyptic temporal perspectives?
Word Studies
  1. What semantic range does the term 'elect' (ἐκλεκτοί) carry in verse 1, and how might its usage here compare to its meaning in canonical texts?
  2. How should we understand the phrase 'took up his parable' in verse 2 - what Hebrew concept (mashal) likely underlies this expression?
  3. What theological significance lies in the title 'Holy Great One' (v3) versus 'eternal God' (v4) - do these reflect different divine attributes or roles?
Literary Features
  1. How does the cosmic imagery in verses 5-7 (shaking mountains, melting hills, rending earth) function rhetorically to prepare readers for the human judgment described in verses 8-9?
  2. What effect does the repetitive use of 'ungodly' four times in verse 9 create, and how does this relate to the Hebrew literary technique of intensification?
  3. How do the passive constructions in verse 6 ('shall be shaken,' 'shall be made low') emphasize divine agency in the coming judgment?

Where this fits in God's big story

Salvation Story
  1. How does Enoch's vision of God coming 'from His dwelling' (v3) to execute judgment connect to the biblical pattern of divine intervention throughout salvation history?
  2. What role does the distinction between 'elect and righteous' (v1) versus 'wicked and godless' play in God's ongoing work of redemption and judgment?
  3. How do the promises in verse 8 that God 'will make peace' and provide 'mercy' for the elect advance the biblical theme of covenant faithfulness?
Christ Connection
  1. How might verse 9's description of one coming 'with ten thousands of His holy ones' to execute judgment prefigure New Testament descriptions of Christ's second coming?
  2. In what ways does verse 8's promise that 'light shall appear unto them' anticipate Jesus' role as the light of the world who brings salvation to God's people?
  3. How does the combination of mercy for the righteous (v8) and judgment on the ungodly (v9) reflect the dual nature of Christ's mission in both advents?
  4. What connections exist between Enoch as 'a righteous man, whose eyes were opened by God' (v2) and Christ as the perfect revealer of the Father?
God's Character
  1. How do verses 4-7's portrayal of God's awesome power and verses 8-9's emphasis on both mercy and judgment reveal the complexity of divine character?
  2. What does verse 8's promise that the elect 'shall all belong to God' reveal about God's desire for relationship with His people throughout redemptive history?
  3. How does God's commitment to 'protect the elect' (v8) while removing 'all the wicked and godless' (v1) demonstrate His covenant loyalty and moral perfection?

Scripture → Observation → Application → Prayer

Scripture
  1. What key phrase or verse from 1 Enoch 1:1-9 stands out most powerfully to you as you read this passage?
  2. Which specific promise to the 'elect and righteous' in verse 8 resonates most deeply with your current spiritual needs?
  3. What aspect of God's character revealed in verses 3-9 captures your attention most strongly today?
Observation
  1. What does verse 2 teach you about how God reveals His plans and purposes to His people across generations?
  2. What do you observe about the relationship between divine judgment and divine mercy based on the contrast between verses 7-9?
  3. How does Enoch's description of his visionary experience in verse 2 illuminate the nature of prophetic revelation?
  4. What pattern do you notice in how verse 8 describes God's multiple blessings upon the elect?
Application
  1. How can you live today as one of the 'elect and righteous' described in verse 1, especially during times of personal tribulation?
  2. What practical steps will you take to experience the peace, protection, and mercy promised to God's people in verse 8?
  3. How does the certainty of God's coming judgment in verse 9 motivate you to examine your own relationship with Him?
  4. In what specific ways can you prepare yourself and others for the 'day of tribulation' mentioned in verse 1?
Prayer
  1. How will you pray for greater understanding of your identity as one of God's 'elect' based on verse 1?
  2. What will you ask God regarding any fear or anxiety you feel about the cosmic upheaval described in verses 5-7?
  3. How will you thank God for the specific promises made to the righteous in verse 8?
  4. What intercession will you offer for those who might be among the 'ungodly' described in verse 9?

A balanced blend drawing from multiple study methods

Observe
  1. What contrasting groups does Enoch repeatedly distinguish throughout verses 1-9, and how are they characterized?
  2. How does the structure shift from blessing (v1) to parable (v2-3) to theophany (v4-7) to judgment (v8-9)?
  3. What specific words or phrases does Enoch use multiple times to describe God's coming intervention?
Context
  1. How does Enoch's claim in verse 2 that this vision is 'not for this generation, but for a remote one' shape the apocalyptic genre of this text?
  2. What significance does Mount Sinai hold in verse 4 as the location where 'the eternal God will tread upon the earth'?
  3. How might the original Aramaic/Hebrew concepts behind 'elect' and 'righteous' in verse 1 inform our understanding of Enoch's audience?
Interpret
  1. What does Enoch mean in verse 3 when he says 'The Holy Great One will come forth from His dwelling' - is this a physical or spiritual manifestation?
  2. How should we understand the cosmic imagery in verses 5-7 where mountains shake and earth melts - literal catastrophe or symbolic judgment?
  3. What is the relationship between God 'making peace' with the righteous (v8) and 'executing judgment' on the ungodly (v9)?
Connect
  1. How does verse 9's description of God coming 'with ten thousands of His holy ones' connect to similar imagery in Deuteronomy 33:2 and Jude 14-15?
  2. What parallels exist between the 'day of tribulation' mentioned in verse 1 and eschatological themes found in Daniel and Revelation?
  3. How does Enoch's role as a 'righteous man, whose eyes were opened by God' (v2) relate to other biblical visionaries like Ezekiel or Isaiah?
Evaluate
  1. What tensions arise between God's mercy toward the elect (v8) and His destruction of 'all that is upon the earth' (v7)?
  2. How do we reconcile Enoch's claim to divine revelation in verse 2 with questions about the canonical status of 1 Enoch?
  3. What assumptions about divine justice underlie the sharp binary between 'elect/righteous' and 'wicked/ungodly' throughout this passage?
Reflect
  1. How does Enoch's vision of ultimate accountability in verse 9 challenge or comfort modern readers facing injustice?
  2. What does it mean for contemporary believers to live as 'elect and righteous' while anticipating a 'day of tribulation' (v1)?
  3. How might the promise that 'light shall appear unto them' (v8) offer hope to Christians experiencing spiritual darkness today?
